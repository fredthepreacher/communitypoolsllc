Pool Service FAQ — Southwest Florida

Direct answers to the questions Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, Venice, and Gulf Coast pool owners ask most. Licensed CPC1459841.

Florida pools require professional service every week, year-round. Florida's year-round heat, intense UV sunlight, and heavy summer rainfall cause chlorine to deplete and algae to grow far faster than in cooler climates. Weekly service keeps water chemistry balanced, equipment running efficiently, and problems caught before they become expensive repairs.

Green water is almost always algae, and Florida's climate creates conditions for it to take hold within 24–48 hours if chlorine levels drop. Year-round heat, intense UV, and phosphates from rain and debris all feed algae growth. Consistent weekly professional service is the most effective prevention.

A complete weekly service includes skimming surface debris, brushing pool walls and steps, vacuuming the floor, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and balancing water chemistry, and a full equipment inspection. Losing more than 1 inch of water per week is a reliable indicator of a pool leak rather than normal evaporation. Use the bucket test: fill a bucket with pool water, place it on a pool step, mark both water levels, and check after 24–48 hours with the pump off. If the pool dropped significantly more than the bucket, a leak is likely.

A variable speed pump runs at multiple speeds — lower for everyday filtration, higher only when needed — using 50–80% less electricity than a single-speed pump. In Florida, where pools run year-round, the energy savings typically pay back the upgrade cost within 1–2 years.

Saltwater pools still use chlorine — the difference is that a salt cell generates it automatically from dissolved salt rather than requiring manual additions. Most Florida pool owners prefer the softer water feel and reduced chemical handling of a salt system.

Gas pool heaters typically last 6–12 years in Florida. Heat pump heaters generally last 10–15 years with proper maintenance. Florida's year-round use means more operating hours than in seasonal climates. Proper water chemistry management significantly affects heater longevity — improper pH damages heat exchangers.

Common pump failure causes in Florida: running the pump with a clogged filter (causes overheating), operating with low water level (causes cavitation), extended UV and heat exposure degrading seals, and electrical issues from lightning — which is the leading cause of electronic equipment failures in Southwest Florida.

After a significant storm: run the filter for 24+ hours to clear debris and cloudy water, check and clean all baskets, test water chemistry (storms dilute stabilizer and alter pH), check equipment for damage, and remove any large debris from the pool immediately. Heavy storm debris loads can consume all available chlorine within hours, so chemistry testing and correction should happen as soon as possible after the storm passes.
