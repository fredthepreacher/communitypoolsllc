Pool Care Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct answers to what Southwest Florida pool owners ask most.

Florida pools require professional service every week, year-round. Florida's year-round heat, intense UV sunlight, and heavy summer rainfall cause chlorine to deplete and algae to grow far faster than in cooler climates. Weekly service keeps water chemistry balanced, equipment running efficiently, and problems caught before they become expensive repairs.

Green water is almost always algae, and Florida's climate creates conditions for it to take hold within 24–48 hours if chlorine levels drop. Year-round heat, intense UV, and phosphates from rain and debris all feed algae growth. Consistent weekly professional service is the most effective prevention. Our green pool recovery service can clear even severe algae blooms.

Weekly pool cleaning in Port Charlotte typically runs $100–$200 per month depending on pool size, condition, and the services included. Community Pools LLC provides clear, upfront pricing. Call 941.258.7529 or use the quote form below for a free estimate tailored to your pool.

A complete weekly service includes skimming surface debris, brushing pool walls and steps, vacuuming the floor, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and balancing water chemistry, and a full equipment inspection. At Community Pools LLC, every visit is a thorough health check — not a quick pass with a net.

A variable speed pump runs at multiple speeds — lower for everyday filtration, higher only when needed — using 50–80% less electricity than a single-speed pump. In Florida, the energy savings typically pay back the upgrade cost within 1–2 years. We install and program variable speed pumps across Southwest Florida.

Saltwater pools still use chlorine — the difference is that a salt cell generates it automatically from dissolved salt. Most Florida pool owners prefer the softer water feel and reduced chemical handling. We install and service salt chlorine generators throughout Southwest Florida.

No. Pools in Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, Venice, and surrounding areas are used year-round and do not need winterization. The only cold-weather adjustment is a simple pump timer setting during rare brief cold snaps to protect equipment.
