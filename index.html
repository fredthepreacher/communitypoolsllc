Pool Care Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Direct answers to what Southwest Florida pool owners ask most.

How often should a pool be serviced in Florida?

Florida pools require professional service every week, year-round. Florida's year-round heat, intense UV sunlight, and heavy summer rainfall cause chlorine to deplete and algae to grow far faster than in cooler climates.

Weekly service keeps water chemistry balanced, equipment running efficiently, and problems caught before they become expensive repairs. Unlike northern states, there is no off-season for pool care in Southwest Florida.

Why does my pool turn green so fast in Florida?

Green water is almost always algae, and Florida's climate creates conditions for it to take hold within 24–48 hours if chlorine levels drop. Year-round heat, intense UV, and phosphates from rain and debris all feed algae growth.

Consistent weekly professional service — keeping chlorine, stabilizer, and phosphate levels in check — is the most effective prevention. Community Pools LLC's green pool recovery service can clear even severe algae blooms.

How much does pool cleaning cost in Port Charlotte, FL?

Weekly pool cleaning in Port Charlotte typically runs $100–$200 per month depending on pool size, condition, and the services included. Pools that require extra chemical treatment or equipment attention may cost more.

Community Pools LLC provides clear, upfront pricing for every pool. Call 941.258.7529 or use the quote form below for a free estimate tailored to your pool.

What is included in weekly pool maintenance?

A complete weekly service includes skimming surface debris, brushing pool walls and steps, vacuuming the floor, emptying skimmer and pump baskets, testing and balancing water chemistry, and a full equipment inspection.

At Community Pools LLC, every visit is a thorough health check — not a quick pass with a net. We document water chemistry readings and flag any equipment concerns so nothing gets missed visit to visit.

How do I know if my pool has a leak?

Losing more than 1 inch of water per week is a reliable indicator of a pool leak rather than normal evaporation. Use the bucket test: fill a bucket with pool water, place it on a pool step, mark both water levels, and check after 24–48 hours with the pump off.

If the pool dropped significantly more than the bucket, a leak is likely. Community Pools LLC performs professional leak detection to locate and repair the source before it causes structural damage.

What is a variable speed pump and why should I upgrade?

A variable speed pump runs at multiple speeds — lower for everyday filtration, higher only when needed — using 50–80% less electricity than a single-speed pump. In Florida, where pools run year-round, the energy savings typically pay back the upgrade cost within 1–2 years.

Community Pools LLC installs and programs variable speed pumps across Southwest Florida. Some Florida utilities offer rebates that help offset part of the installation cost.

Is a saltwater pool better than a chlorine pool?

Saltwater pools still use chlorine — the difference is that a salt cell generates it automatically from dissolved salt rather than requiring manual additions. Most Florida pool owners prefer the softer water feel and reduced chemical handling of a salt system.

Salt cells require cleaning and eventual replacement every 3–7 years, but eliminate the need to handle chlorine tablets or liquid. Community Pools LLC installs and services salt chlorine generators throughout Southwest Florida.

Do I need to winterize my pool in Southwest Florida?

No. Pools in Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, Venice, and surrounding areas are used year-round and do not need winterization. Traditional winterization is for climates where pipes can freeze — which does not apply in Southwest Florida.

The only cold-weather adjustment is a simple pump timer setting during rare brief cold snaps to protect equipment. Your pool stays in full service every month of the year.
