Why You Need a Licensed Pool Repair Contractor in Florida

Florida law requires a Certified Pool/Spa Contractor (CPC) license for pool repairs. This is not a formality — it exists because improper pool electrical work is a significant safety hazard, improper plumbing repairs can lead to water loss and foundation issues, and unlicensed structural work may not be covered by homeowner's insurance.

Community Pools LLC holds license CPC1459841 issued by the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Our technicians are trained to work safely with pool electrical systems, understand the specific plumbing requirements for Florida pools, and can pull permits when required for significant repairs.

Unlicensed pool repair operators are common in Southwest Florida. They may charge less upfront, but their work creates liability for the homeowner, may void equipment warranties, and frequently introduces new problems by fixing the symptom without addressing the underlying cause. A pump that keeps failing every few months isn't a pump problem — it's a hydraulic or electrical problem that requires a licensed diagnosis.

We provide written estimates before beginning any repair work. There are no surprise charges and no upselling of parts you don't need. If a repair is not cost-effective compared to replacement, we'll tell you that directly.

Common Pool Repair Questions

How do I know if my pool pump needs repair or replacement?

A pool pump typically warrants repair if it is less than 5–7 years old and the repair cost is less than 50% of a new pump. Common repairable issues include worn capacitors, failed seals, and clogged impellers. Replacement is typically the better choice when the motor is more than 8 years old, has repeated failures, or the repair cost approaches or exceeds replacement cost. We give you an honest recommendation based on age, condition, and repair economics — not on which option earns us more.

Why does my pool pump make a loud noise?

Loud pump noise most commonly indicates one of these issues: air in the system (gurgling or cavitation), a failing bearing in the motor (grinding or screeching), a clogged impeller (straining hum), or vibration from loose mounting hardware. Each has a different cause and fix. Operating a noisy pump without diagnosis risks accelerating the failure and potentially damaging the motor beyond repair. Call 941.258.7529 for a diagnosis.

Can pool plumbing be repaired without draining the pool?

Many pool plumbing repairs above the water line can be completed without draining the pool. Above-ground unions, valves, pump connections, and return lines can typically be repaired with the pool at normal water level. Underground pipe repairs usually require locating the break precisely (sometimes with leak detection equipment) and may require partial draining for access. We assess each situation and use the least-invasive approach possible.

How long does a typical pool repair take?

Simple repairs like a pump seal replacement or multiport valve repair can often be completed in 1–2 hours during a single visit. More complex repairs requiring special-order parts, permits, or multiple trades (like a heater replacement or underground plumbing repair) may take 3–7 business days from diagnosis to completion. We provide a time estimate alongside the cost estimate so you can plan accordingly.
