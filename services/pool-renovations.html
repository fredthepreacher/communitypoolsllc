A renovation addresses all of these simultaneously and resets your pool for another 15–20 years of low-maintenance use.

Signs Your Pool Is Ready for Renovation

Most pools in Charlotte County were built 15–30 years ago. The boom-era construction that defined Port Charlotte and Punta Gorda's residential growth means a large percentage of pools in the area are approaching or have passed their original surface and equipment lifespan.

Common indicators that a renovation is the right decision rather than continued repair: plaster that is rough to the touch, visibly pitted, or persistently staining despite correct chemistry; a concrete deck that has cracked, settled, or developed trip hazards; tile that is consistently coming loose or showing significant scale despite regular acid washing; equipment that requires increasingly frequent repairs; or a pool that simply looks dated compared to modern standards.

Travertine paver decks have become the most popular renovation choice among Southwest Florida homeowners in recent years, and for good reason. Natural travertine is significantly cooler underfoot than concrete in Florida's summer sun, it's aesthetically superior, and its individual paver installation means cracked sections can be replaced individually rather than requiring full deck replacement. The travertine before-and-after results we show in our project gallery consistently represent the most dramatic visual transformations in our portfolio.

Renovating surface, deck, and equipment simultaneously is substantially more cost-efficient than addressing each in separate projects over time. The pool is drained once, access scaffolding is set up once, the new plaster cure period overlaps with deck curing — and the result is a unified, modern pool that looks and functions like new.

Pool Renovation FAQ

How long does pool resurfacing take in Florida?

Pool replastering typically takes 7–14 days from draining to swim-ready. The plaster application itself takes 1–2 days, followed by a refill period (typically 24–36 hours depending on pool size), and then a startup chemistry period of 5–10 days during which the plaster cures and the water is carefully balanced. Full deck renovation combined with replastering can extend the total timeline to 14–21 days depending on scope and weather.

How much does pool resurfacing cost in Port Charlotte?

Pool replastering in Port Charlotte and Southwest Florida typically costs $4,000–$8,000 for a standard residential pool with white plaster or quartz aggregate finish. Premium pebble finishes cost more. Travertine deck renovation adds $6,000–$15,000+ depending on the deck area. Full renovation including surface, deck, and equipment can range from $15,000–$35,000+ depending on scope. Call 941.258.7529 for a free on-site estimate for your specific pool.

Is travertine or concrete better for a Florida pool deck?

Travertine pavers are generally the superior choice for Florida pool decks for three reasons: (1) natural travertine stays significantly cooler than concrete in full Florida sun — up to 30°F cooler surface temperature; (2) travertine's individual paver installation means individual cracked or stained pieces can be replaced without full deck removal; (3) the aesthetics are consistently preferred by homeowners. Concrete is less expensive upfront but tends to crack more frequently in Florida's soil conditions and can't be partially replaced. Travertine provides better long-term value for most homeowners.

Do I need a permit for pool renovation in Florida?

In Florida, permits are typically required for pool resurfacing, significant structural work, equipment replacement, and deck construction. Community Pools LLC pulls all required permits as part of the renovation process — it's included in the project management, not an add-on cost. Permitted work ensures the renovation is inspected to code, protects your home's insurance coverage, and avoids disclosure complications when you sell the property.
