Signs Your Pool Has a Leak

Normal pool water loss from evaporation and splash-out in Florida's climate is roughly ¼ to ½ inch per day. A leak produces water loss significantly beyond that level — but distinguishing a leak from high evaporation requires a systematic approach, not a guess.

Community Pools LLC uses proven detection methods to locate leaks precisely before any repair work begins. Finding the exact location is critical: unnecessary deck excavation or plumbing replacement to fix the wrong area wastes money and introduces new potential failure points.

Why Pool Leaks Are Serious in Florida

A leaking pool in Southwest Florida isn't just wasting water — it's wasting chemicals, potentially undermining your pool's foundation, and quietly eroding the soil under your deck and equipment pad. Charlotte County's sandy soil is particularly vulnerable to erosion caused by continual water loss from an undetected underground plumbing leak.

A pool losing ½ inch of water per day beyond normal evaporation loses approximately 10,000–15,000 gallons per month. That water carries your chemicals with it, dramatically increasing your chemical costs as you continuously try to maintain chemistry in a pool that's constantly being diluted. A slow leak that goes unrepaired for 6–12 months can cost hundreds of dollars in wasted water and chemicals, on top of the repair cost itself.

Underground plumbing leaks are the most difficult to detect and the most damaging over time. The water doesn't disappear — it saturates the soil under your deck and pool shell. Over months and years, this can cause deck settling, crack formation in pool shells, and in severe cases, structural damage requiring significant renovation. Early detection and repair is always significantly less expensive than addressing the downstream consequences of a missed leak.

Florida also has specific water-use regulations. During drought conditions and water restrictions, significant unexplained water loss may attract utility attention. More practically, a leaking pool that causes property damage to your yard, neighbor's property, or home foundation can create liability beyond just the repair bill.

Pool Leak Detection FAQ

How do I know if my pool is leaking or just evaporating?

The bucket test is the standard method: fill a bucket with pool water, place it on a step in the pool so it's the same temperature as the pool water, mark both the bucket water level and the pool water level, and check after 24 hours. If the pool loses significantly more water than the bucket, a leak is likely. Normal evaporation loss in Florida is typically ¼–½ inch per day. Loss of 1 inch or more per day with the pump running (or any loss with the pump off) strongly indicates a leak.

Can pool leaks be repaired without draining the pool?

Many pool leaks can be repaired without fully draining the pool. Surface cracks, fitting leaks, and skimmer repairs can often be done at normal water level or with a partial drain. Underground plumbing repairs typically require accessing the pipe section, which may involve targeted excavation rather than a full pool drain. Shell repairs that require surface preparation and curing may require a full or partial drain depending on the crack location. We use the least-invasive approach that allows a reliable repair.

Where do pools most commonly leak?

The most common pool leak locations are: (1) fittings and light niches in the shell, where caulk or putty dries and shrinks over time; (2) the skimmer-to-shell connection, especially in older pools; (3) underground return or suction pipe joints, which can shift as soil settles; (4) pump and equipment plumbing unions and O-rings; and (5) structural cracks in the pool shell, more common in older plaster pools or pools that have experienced significant ground movement. Florida's sandy soil movement contributes to fitting leaks over time.
