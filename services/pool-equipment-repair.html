Equipment Pad Upgrades for Florida Pools

If your pool's equipment is aging — 8–15 years old is common in Charlotte County's established neighborhoods — a full equipment pad upgrade may be more cost-effective than repeated repairs. Modern pool equipment delivers significant advantages over equipment installed even 10 years ago.

Variable-speed pumps are the highest-impact upgrade available to most Florida pool owners. Replacing a single-speed pump with a variable-speed model typically reduces pool-related electricity costs by 60–80%, pays for itself in 2–3 years through energy savings, and provides better water quality through continuous low-flow filtration. They're also quieter than single-speed pumps and come with variable warranties of up to 3 years.

Salt chlorination eliminates the need to handle and store liquid chlorine, provides softer-feeling water, and reduces overall chemical costs once the system is dialed in. Combined with an automation system, your entire pool can be managed from a smartphone — adjusting temperature, lighting, jets, and sanitization from anywhere.

Community Pools LLC provides full equipment pad replacement: concrete pad work, new equipment installation, electrical connection, and startup. All work is performed to Florida building code, and permits are pulled when required. After installation, we program and optimize every component for your specific pool's size, volume, and usage patterns.

Pool Equipment Repair FAQ

How long do pool pumps last in Florida?

A well-maintained pool pump in Florida typically lasts 8–12 years. Florida's year-round operation means Florida pumps run more total hours than pumps in seasonal climates, which accelerates wear. Variable-speed pumps often outlast single-speed pumps because they run at lower RPM most of the time, reducing heat and bearing wear. Poor water chemistry, restricted flow from clogged filters, and low water level operation all significantly reduce pump lifespan.

Is it worth repairing a pool heater or should I replace it?

Pool heater repair is generally worth it if the unit is less than 8–10 years old and the repair cost is under 40–50% of a new heater. Common repairable issues include ignition system failures, bypass valves, and control boards. Heat exchangers that have corroded through due to improper water chemistry are typically not worth repairing. If a gas heater is more than 12 years old with multiple issues, replacement with a high-efficiency unit usually makes more financial sense over a 5-year horizon.

How much electricity does a variable-speed pool pump save?

A variable-speed pump typically reduces pool pump electricity costs by 60–80% compared to a single-speed pump. A single-speed 1.5 HP pump running 8 hours per day costs roughly $80–$120 per month in Florida electricity rates. The same circulation task accomplished by a variable-speed pump at optimal RPM settings typically costs $15–$30 per month. The pump typically pays for itself within 2–3 years through energy savings alone, and Florida utility rebates may be available to reduce the upfront cost further.

My pool automation is not responding — what could be wrong?

Pool automation failures have several common causes: a tripped circuit breaker (check the panel first), a control board failure, a failed communication wire between the pad and indoor control panel, a firmware issue requiring a factory reset, or a failed relay. Lightning is a frequent cause of automation failures in Florida — nearby strikes can damage control boards and sensors even with surge protection. Most automation manufacturers support remote diagnostics, which we can use to narrow down the issue before coming on-site.
